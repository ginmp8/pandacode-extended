## Ten highest-risk gaps

1. **Required Council is native but not product-reachable.** The adapter at `src-tauri/src/superagent/council_adapter.rs:745-1372` is real and tested in source, but Team Builder has no required-Council control and manifest v2 has no field. Every Council-required acceptance row therefore rests on native fixtures, not a shipped journey. Worse, any Council-enabled Team exported through manifest v2 and reimported is silently downgraded to Council-off because the schema has no required flag and `apply()` hard-codes the snapshot flag false. This is the largest separation between "code exists" and "user can obtain the behavior", and it is a quality-gate downgrade risk.
2. **No work-item graph or scheduler.** Ticket and Assignment are the only durable units (`src-tauri/src/superagent.rs:88-132`). There is no dependency DAG, ready frontier, automatic stage transition or parallel item execution. The autonomous-delivery rows and the Runs UX cannot be satisfied by UI work alone.
3. **No authenticated GitHub delivery and no Auto.** Sources are Off or Observe only, with a user-clicked one-shot fetch. The schema cannot store Auto, the Ticket source can only be `manual`, and caller-supplied envelopes are rejected. Uniform intake, subscriptions, Auto admission, policy revocation and source-driven recovery are all absent.
4. **Headless lifecycle is incomplete.** Serve can list, inspect, accept, reject, stop and comment, but cannot create, admit, retry, resume or recover. Desktop and headless parity is therefore structurally impossible today.
5. **External effects cover one class only.** The dispatcher is an issue-comment path. Branches, commits, issues, PRs, reviews and final replies have no grant, idempotency, read-back or reconciliation contract.
6. **Skills state and provisioning are missing.** Role snapshots capture a bot's Skills JSON, but there is no library-installed, assigned, harness-eligible, task-available, invoked or evidence-verified distinction, no missing-skill block and no Skills UI. Required review evidence can be absent without a truthful product state.
7. **Team policy is largely hard-coded.** Autonomy is always off, max concurrency is one and attempts are two (`src-tauri/src/superagent.rs:645-652`). Per-role models, harnesses, Skills, fallbacks, budgets, token and cost limits and optimization strategies cannot be configured, so Economy and Performance Teams are not real product concepts yet.
8. **Nested subagents are entirely absent.** There is no child record, grant subset, depth, quota, lineage, join, drain or UI tree. The full nested-delegation addendum is unimplemented, and the shared Architect child mechanism is a different subsystem.
9. **Exact-SHA verification is not available.** CI is reported red, no local Rust or Playwright check ran, no before or after screenshots exist, and the HEAD docs are stale: the source map reports schema 46 versus 51 and a one-worker lifecycle, while the status prose still calls the committed Council adapter advisory. This ledger is a drift map, not an acceptance proof.
10. **Scoped writes are narrow and a restart test is reported failing.** Writes are Windows plus Codex only, Unix fails closed, there is no general tool grant, and the HEAD docs report one SuperAgent effects restart test failing without a diagnosed cause. This blocks any claim that authorized worker writes and restart recovery are production-ready across supported targets.
